01Essence

Earned, not advertised.

Montec belongs to the "old money" tradition of luxury: value carried quietly, legible only to those who know what they are looking at. The logo is small. The leather is the loudest thing in the room, and it never raises its voice. Our customer is the founder, the financier, the lawyer — someone whose success is already established and who wants an object that reflects it without announcing it.

We are not affordable luxury. We are earned luxury — the world's finest materials, made at home in Armenia, offered only to those who ask.

Three words

Quiet. Restraint over spectacle. We remove before we add.

Permanent. Made to outlast trends and, ideally, their owner. Leather that improves with age.

Ours. Armenian craft carried with pride, at home and across the diaspora.

“I am not rich enough to buy cheap things.”

The line for the object itself — why the price is the price.

“Craftsmanship and strategy for those who write history.”

03Logo & Wordmark

One wordmark. One monogram. Infinite restraint.

The wordmark

MONTEC set in Cormorant Garamond, medium weight, with generous 0.22em letter-spacing. The tracking is the signature — it turns six letters into a piece of architecture. Always upper-case. Never condensed, never italic, never re-drawn.

The monogram

Two overlapping triangular peaks forming a stylized mountain silhouette — the "Mont" of Montec, made visible. Used where the wordmark cannot fit: the blind-embossed leather mark, the engraved brass rivet, the favicon, the wax seal. This is the only mark that ever touches the product, and it is embossed tonally — felt more than seen, never inked or foiled in contrast.

Clear space & the rules

Give the wordmark clear space equal to the cap-height of the M on every side. Minimum digital width 120px; minimum print width 26mm. Only three colourways exist: near-black on paper, paper on near-black, and the tonal brass emboss. No shadows, no gradients on the mark, no outline, no stretching.

Don'ts

×

Don't stretch

Never distort the proportions of the wordmark or the mark. Scale uniformly, always.

×

Don't change tracking or weight

The 0.22em letter-spacing and medium weight are the wordmark. Tighter or bolder is a different brand.

×

Don't fill with colour

No ink, no foil, no gradient contrast on the product mark — blind emboss, tone-on-tone, always. This is the one rule that never bends.

×

Don't redraw the peaks

The two overlapping triangles are fixed geometry. No rounding, no simplifying, no "improving."

×

Don't place on clutter

Busy photography or low-contrast grounds need a solid clearspace pad behind the mark — never float it bare.

×

Don't default to foil

Gold foil is for hero and cover imagery only. Every-day product branding is blind emboss, not foil.

04Colour

Near-black and brass. Almost nothing else.

A disciplined, three-plus-one palette. Near-black is the ground, brass is the only accent, anthracite gives it air, warm paper is where the words live. Colour is used with the same restraint as everything else: one accent, never five.

05Typography

A serif that carries the name. A sans that gets out of the way.

Display · Cormorant Garamond
Quiet is the loudest luxury.
Headlines · the wordmark · pull-quotes · product names

Text · Inter
Everything you need to read, and nothing you notice.
Body copy · captions · interface · specifications · the fine print of a serious brand.
Weights in use: 300 / 400 / 500 · Aa Bb Cc 0123456789 · set generously, never crowded.

Pairing rule

Cormorant carries emotion and heritage; Inter carries information. Never set long body copy in the serif, and never set the wordmark or a product name in the sans. The tension between the two — old and exact — is the typographic identity.

Spacing rule

Space is a luxury material. Headlines breathe with tight leading and wide tracking; body copy is set at a comfortable 62–66 characters. When in doubt, remove an element and add air.

06Voice & Tone

Restrained, literate, unhurried, certain.

We speak the way our customer does: fewer words, chosen well. We never hype, never exclaim, never chase. The brand is doing the reader a courtesy by admitting them — that quiet confidence is the whole tone. We write short declarative sentences and let silence do the rest.

We say
  • The price is the price.
  • Made at home, from the world's finest leather.
  • Owned by those who ask.
  • It will outlast the trend, and probably you.
  • Quiet is the loudest luxury.
We never say
  • Sale · Discount · Limited-time offer
  • Luxurious!! · Amazing deal · Must-have
  • Buy now · Don't miss out · Hurry
  • Cheap · Affordable · Budget
  • Anything with an exclamation mark.

08Packaging

The unboxing is the first handshake.

The box

Rigid, matte near-black, blind-embossed monogram on the lid. No print, no colour, no cellophane. Weight and restraint communicate before it is even opened.

Inside

Cotton dust bag, a warm-paper card with the batch number and the owner's name, and the piece itself. The card is letterpress, brass foil monogram only.

The gift edition

For corporate programmes: the recipient's initials engraved, a personal card, the same box. A gift that makes the giver look as good as the brand.

Principle: a customer should feel the money in the packaging before they see the product. It is the cheapest luxury we can buy and the one they remember.

09Art Direction

Show the grain. Show the life. Never the price.

Photography

Low, directional light on near-black grounds. Extreme macro on the grain, the stitch, the burnished edge, the brass being set. The leather is always the hero and is shot close enough to touch. Warm shadows, never clinical.

Motion

Cinematic vertical 9:16. The cut, the closing of a deal, the patina after years. Every reel opens on a single striking frame and carries one idea. Craft in silence, or a single line of the Code over a slow shot.

The world we depict

The desk, the boardroom, the lounge, the handshake — aspirational but plausible. When Armenian identity appears, it is pride and subtlety, never folklore. The viewer should leave not thinking "I should buy that" but "that is who I want to be seen as."

Never

No bright backgrounds, no clutter, no stock-photo smiles, no price overlays, no sale stickers, no trend props. If a frame could belong to any brand, it does not belong to Montec.

10Brand Pattern

Contour lines. The mountain, read as topography.

A single decorative texture: a fine brass hairline grid on obsidian, wider verticals crossing tighter horizontals — quiet enough to read as texture, structured enough to nod at a topographic map without literally drawing one. It is a whisper, not a wallpaper.

When to use

As a background texture behind large clearspace on packaging interiors, the inside cover of print documents, and digital section dividers — always behind a dominant headline or logo, never as the main subject.

When to avoid

Never on product pages behind hero photography, never at full opacity, never combined with the gold-foil logo treatment — pick one quiet gesture per surface, not two.
