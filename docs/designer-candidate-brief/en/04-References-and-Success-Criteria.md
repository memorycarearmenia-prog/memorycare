# References and success criteria

## The first 5 seconds on the site

The person should:

- **Understand**: "professional care for memorial plots in Armenia, with
  photo/video proof of every visit."
- **Feel**: calm and trust, not sadness or anxiety.
- **See**: a sample before/after report — right on the first screen, not
  buried inside the site.

## Measurable success criteria (a reference point, not a hard KPI for the designer)

- Visitor → lead conversion: ≥3%.
- Lead → payment conversion: ≥40% (this is no longer a design metric,
  but design does influence the trust that drives conversion).
- Mobile load time: <3 seconds.
- Mobile conversion should be no worse than desktop.

## Tone and approach references

- **tending.app** — a direct category analog, calm tone.
- **headspace.com** — calm and lightness without funeral aesthetics.
- **stripe.com** — cleanliness, trust in the payment section.
- **airbnb.com** — a card-based, photo-first approach to content.

## What to avoid (repeated from the brand section, because it's critical)

- Dominant black, crosses, gothic fonts, candles — funeral clichés that
  are explicitly rejected.
- Guilt-tripping in copy or visuals.
- Excessive sentimentality.
- Cold corporate style.
- Cheap stock instead of real (even if currently temporary, but clean)
  material.
- Loud discount elements, ticking countdown timers, etc. — this is a
  premium service, not a clearance sale.
- Diaspora-exclusive copy and visuals ("across the ocean," "cheaper than
  flying yourself") — the site should work equally well for a Yerevan
  client.
- The word "monthly" in relation to the "Maximum" tier — it's 9 visits,
  not 12, and it's not a calendar subscription.
- The word "bestseller" in client-facing materials — the leading tier
  needs a more understated way of standing out.
- A QR code on the headstone / "digital memory page" — that's a product
  for the company's next stage of growth; it is not part of the current
  brief and must not appear on the site, even as "coming soon."

## Delivery format

- Mockups — first priority for the three screens listed in
  `00-Overview.md` (report template, pricing page, cabinet screens),
  everything else as a second stage.
- Source files (Figma/AI) are handed over to the company as a condition
  of each stage's acceptance.
- The final call on the visual direction rests with the company, but if
  you have a well-reasoned professional disagreement with a detail of
  the brief, you're welcome to propose alternatives.
