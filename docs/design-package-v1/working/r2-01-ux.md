# 1. Conflicts, with rulings

## C1. Pricing page structure — three-way disagreement, and the brief itself is wrong

**Positions.** 02 puts a row of three Ivory cards — Express · Optimal · Maximum — with Inspection as a full-width band above and Special as a ruled line (and flags this as unresolved in its B.3). 05 encodes exactly that in code (`TariffCard` 3-up at `lg`, Inspection as `TariffCard--standalone`) but then adds **Special as a fifth card in the row's footer position**. 03 is internally contradictory: §A.3 heads a section "The three annual subscriptions" and then puts **four** cards under it — Express, Optimal, Maximum, Special — while its own §0 rule 8 and §C.3 demand two bands (one-offs: Inspection + Express; annual: Optimal + Maximum + Special). 04 rules Special out of the row entirely and puts both one-offs in a band above. I proposed "three subscription cards", which is simply a miscount.

**What breaks.** A three-card row containing Express misrepresents a one-off as an annual tier — the exact confusion the brief's "Inspection must read as a one-off" rule exists to prevent, and it makes the credit rule a footnote on two unrelated cards. Special as a card adds a fifth thing to compare with no price on it, which slows every visitor to serve a minority. Conversely, demoting Express out of card status contradicts DECISION 3, which insists Express is a real product at 65,000, not a lead-in.

**Ruling — two bands, and the brief's sentence is amended.**

```
Band A  ONE-OFF SERVICES        Inspection 20,000 ֏ AMD  ·  Express 65,000 ֏ AMD
        surface-sunken, horizontal at md+, no "/ year" line, equal price type size
        → credit rule stated once, in full, directly beneath this band (C4 below)

Band B  ANNUAL SUBSCRIPTIONS    Optimal 160,000 ֏ AMD / year (leading)  ·  Maximum 200,000 ֏ AMD / year
        surface-raised cards, 2-up at md+, 1-up below

Special  a single ruled line beneath the calculator. No card, no price.
         "Larger plot, several family plots, or a case that does not fit?
          We price it after an Inspection." → CTA: Start with an Inspection
```

**Reason.** Only Optimal and Maximum are annual. The brief's phrase "apart from the three annual subscriptions" is factually false and is what generated three incompatible layouts. Two bands is the only arrangement in which no card is ever compared across a boundary, and it makes the credit rule a property of the band that earns it. Adopting 04's "fork" framing on top of this is optional; the band structure is not.

**Consequences to file:** 05 changes `TariffCard` grid to 2-up at `lg` (not 3-up) and deletes the "Special is a fifth card" rule; `TariffCard--standalone` becomes `OneOffBand` holding two items. 02's home-page block 6 and 03's §A.3 headings both need rewriting.

**Owner ratification needed** — this edits one sentence of the approved brief (§5). See §5 of this memo.

## C2. Home page pricing — cards or a list

**Positions.** 02 and I both put a card row on Home; 03 puts a compact four-line price list (Block 7).

**Ruling: 03 wins.** Home carries the four named products as four lines with prices and one CTA to `/pricing/`. **Reason:** duplicating the card component on Home forces us to duplicate the one-off/annual band split too, doubling the maintenance surface on the page that most needs to stay short, and the local buyer (Armen, 40 seconds) reads a four-line list faster than four cards. I was wrong.

## C3. The report block order — four different orders

**Positions.** Mine: crew note before photographs. 02: confirmation → GPS → work done → photographs → crew note → next visit. 03: confirmation → GPS → photographs → video → crew note → recommended work → share. 05: status → plot line → GPS chip → photographs → notes → video → actions, with **GPS demoted to a chip** and "first image = the plot AFTER the visit".

**What breaks.** 05's demotion of GPS to a chip removes the single element that answers the master objection ("will they actually go?", 04's objections 5 and 6). My placement of the crew note before photographs makes the reader wade through prose to reach the thing they opened the report for.

**Ruling — one canonical order, `ReportCard`:**

```
1  Masthead          mark · "Visit report" · plot identity · cemetery      [guest: yes]
2  Confirmation      "The visit took place" · date · status · arrival/departure
                     + GPS element (its own block, not a chip) — see C7      [guest: yes]
3  Work performed    ticked list, max 8, first 4 + "show all" on mobile      [guest: yes]
4  Photographs       "On arrival" group, then "After the work" group          [guest: yes]
5  Video             optional, poster frame, never autoplay                   [guest: yes]
6  Crew note         the one first-person voice in the product               [guest: yes]
7  Recommended work  Owner + Family manager only, prices, changed ground     [guest: NO]
8  Documents         PDF                                                      [guest: yes]
9  Actions           Share · Order additional work · Request a re-visit       [guest: text link only]
10 Next visit        date                                                     [guest: NO]
```

I concede the crew-note position to 02/03/05 (three to one, and the note reads better as commentary on images already seen). 05 concedes the GPS block. 05's "first image = the plot AFTER the visit" is kept: the after-image opens the gallery, the arrival group is labelled and sits second — which is the same rule 02 states as "in sequence, vertically". Nobody's before/after slider survives; all four proposals independently forbid it.

## C4. Where the credit rule lives

**Positions.** 04 wants it on the lower rung — on the Inspection and Express cards themselves. 03 wants it once, as a four-bullet block under the cards. 02 does not place it. I put it in a three-row table in a separate pricing section.

**Ruling: both, and only these two places.** One short line on each one-off card ("Credited toward an annual subscription signed within 60 days"), and the full four-rule block immediately beneath Band A — never behind a tooltip, never a footnote. 04's argument (the client reads the risk-removal at the moment of hesitation) and 03's (this is the rule most likely to produce an angry email) are both right and are not in conflict. The full block uses 03's wording verbatim; 04's longer paragraph version is cut.

## C5. Header — button or phone

**Positions.** 04: "Header: a phone number, not a button." 02 and 05: desktop header carries a Deep Olive primary CTA button plus nav plus language switcher; mobile carries a call button and a menu button with the CTA in the sticky bar. Mine: button plus phone icon.

**Ruling: 02/05 structure, with 04's mobile instinct honoured.**
- Desktop (`lg`+): lock-up · 5 nav items · language switcher · `Request a consultation` (primary).
- Mobile (`base`–`md`): lock-up · 44×44 tap-to-call · 44×44 menu. No CTA button in the header; it lives in the `MobileActionBar`.

**Reason.** 04's point is about mobile, where it is correct and is already satisfied. Removing the desktop button costs conversions from the local buyer who is on a laptop at work and will not phone from an open-plan office.

## C6. Consent checkbox on the consultation form

**Positions.** 04 explicitly rejects "any consent checkbox beyond a single-line privacy statement". 03 and I require one.

**Ruling: the checkbox stays**, as one line with a link, worded per 03: *"I agree to MemoryCare contacting me about this request."* **Reason.** A material share of this audience is resident in France and the wider EU; the bank's package requires an English privacy policy and a demonstrable lawful basis; and the cost is one tap against a lawsuit and a bank-review failure. 04 is right that every field costs requests — which is why we spend the tap here and nowhere else (see C10).

## C7. Shadows

**Positions.** 02: "There is not one shadow in the system," elevation is a ground change plus a hairline, modals use a scrim. 05: a five-step elevation ramp with shadows up to `0 20px 48px rgba(51,55,60,0.12)` on modals.

**Ruling: 02 wins for the page; 05 keeps two.** No shadow on any card, tariff card, report sheet, input, badge or band. Shadows exist only on (a) the modal/sheet, where a scrim alone does not lift an Ivory dialog off a Nude page convincingly on a phone, and (b) the drawer. `mc.elevation.1/2/3` are deleted from the token file; `elevation.4` is renamed `mc.elevation.overlay`. **Reason.** Shadow is the SaaS-template tell, and this brand's whole visual argument is "paper on stone". A hairline plus the 1.1 Nude/Ivory step does the work everywhere it is asked to.

## C8. Ivory as a full-bleed band

**Positions.** 05: "Ivory is never a page background. Nude is never a card background" — testable, lintable. 02: full-bleed Ivory bands for "document moments" (sample report, calculator, legal pages), with the nesting rule that a card inside an Ivory band loses its fill.

**Ruling: 02's device survives, under a named token.** Add `mc.surface.band-document` = Ivory, permitted **only** on the sample-report band, the calculator band and the legal-page body. The 05 rule is otherwise absolute and the linter enforces it, with these three routes as declared exceptions. 02's nesting rule ("no Ivory object on an Ivory band — hairline only") is adopted as a system rule. **Reason.** The device is the strongest thing in 02's concept and it is cheap; an unnamed exception is what actually breaks, so it gets a token and a whitelist.

## C9. Two fixed bars on the same mobile viewport — impossible as specified

02 pins the calculator result panel to the bottom of the viewport as a 96px bar while the sliders are in view. 02, 04, 05 and I all also pin a sticky CTA bar (56 / 64 / 72px, three different numbers). At 360×640 that is up to 168px of fixed chrome — a quarter of the screen — and the two bars occupy the same coordinates.

**Ruling.** The `MobileActionBar` is **suppressed** on `/pricing/` while the calculator is in the viewport (`IntersectionObserver`), and the calculator result panel takes the bar position. Everywhere else the action bar is present. One fixed bar at the block-end, ever. Height is **64px + `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`** — 56 is too tight for a 48px button with padding, 72 eats too much of a 640px viewport. 05's rule "hides while a form field has focus" is adopted.

# 3. Gaps — what a developer hits on day one and nobody wrote down

**G1. The credit and the pro-rata refund contradict each other, and the arithmetic loses money.**
A client pays Express 65,000, subscribes to Optimal, is credited, and pays 95,000 for the year. Four visits are due. After one visit they cancel. Every proposal says "we refund the visits you have not received, pro rata" — 3 of 4 unreceived, at a list price of 160,000, is **120,000 ֏ refunded against 95,000 ֏ received.** No document says which figure the arithmetic runs on. **Ruling to be written into the refund policy, the cancellation screen and `products.json`:** refund = **amount actually paid for the subscription × (visits not delivered ÷ visits in the plan)**, rounded **up** to the nearest 100 ֏ in the client's favour. Worked example above: 95,000 × 3/4 = 71,250 → **71,300 ֏**. Maximum divides by 6 and will produce fractions on every cancellation; the rounding rule is not optional. This is a finance ruling as much as a design one and it blocks both the bank package and the portal build.

**G2. Pro-rata basis: by visits, not by days.** I flagged this in round one as unanswered. 03 answers it twice, independently and identically ("we refund the visits you have paid for and not yet received"), and 04 does the same. **Three of us agree; take it as settled: by visits delivered.** It is comprehensible, and it never tells a client who has had one of four visits that they consumed 27% of the year.

**G3. What a "subscription year" is — nobody defined it.** Optimal is "4 visits, one per season". A client who signs on 20 November: do they get four visits in the twelve months from signature, or four visits in the calendar seasons, the first of which is three months away? This determines the schedule on the portal first-entry screen (the screen 04 and I both call the highest-risk moment in the product, and it is empty without a date), the renewal date, and the pro-rata denominator. **Ruling: rolling twelve months from the date the subscription is signed; visits are scheduled into seasonal windows within it, shown as month ranges until dated.** Owner to confirm the operational reality.

**G4. The plot has no identity model.** No document except mine names a field for cemetery, sector, row, coordinates or the name on the monument — yet the report masthead, the guest link, the invitation email ("{plot_label}"), the crew's navigation and the OG string all consume it. 03 uses `{plot_label}` seventeen times without ever defining it. **Ruling: `plot { cemetery, sector, row, coordinates, display_name, display_mode }`, where `display_mode ∈ {family_name, full_name, none}` is chosen once by the Owner at onboarding, defaults to `family_name`, and is editable in the portal.** Displaying a dead person's name is a consent decision, not a design one.

**G5. The share link is a public URL and nobody wrote its security rules.** Four documents specify the `/r/` guest view; none specifies token entropy, indexing or expiry. **Ruling:** ≥128 bits of entropy, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` plus a `noindex` meta on the route, revocable from the share sheet, non-expiring by default, dead on subscription cancellation. Note the consequence nobody has faced: **rendering an OG card means Meta's and Viber's crawlers fetch that URL unauthenticated.** That is fine only because the OG image is a static generated asset and the description carries no plot data — see G6.

**G6. The OG description leaks the cemetery.** 03's `og:description` is *"A record of a MemoryCare visit to a family memorial plot in {cemetery}…"*. The brief (§8) says the preview is *"Mark, 'Visit report', date only."* **Ruling: date only.** `og:title = "Visit report — {date}"`, `og:description = "A record of a MemoryCare visit. Photographs, video and GPS confirmation."` — no cemetery, no plot label, no name. The link is forwarded past the family more often than the family thinks. The OG **image ground is Anthracite**, not Nude (03 says Nude; the colour mark's hands vanish on Nude — the defect named in the brief). 02 and 05 both have this right.

**G7. Transactional email is unspecified and undesignable from what we have.** Six emails exist as copy in 03 (report ready, visit tomorrow, postponed, no access, payment received, renewal) and 04 adds two more (payment confirmation within minutes, activation). None of them has a template, and **none of the design system applies**: email clients do not support CSS custom properties, `clamp()`, `dvh`, webfonts reliably, or `:focus-visible`. **Ruling: a separate one-column 600px table-based email template, inline styles, literal hex values generated from the tokens at build time, Georgia/Arial fallbacks for Gloock/Cabin, no background images.** Owner: the design system engineer, not Igor. This is the artefact the diaspora client sees before they ever reach the portal, and it is currently nobody's.

**G8. The report PDF is offered four times and specified nowhere.** Same block order, A4, **never any price in any variant** (so one file serves owner, member and guest), the tagline set from the print asset, and the ֏ glyph problem below applies to it too. Who generates it (server) is Igor's; what it contains is ours.

**G9. The bank-transfer invoice does not exist as a design object.** 04 flags it; nobody designs it. It is the only artefact between "consultation" and "paid client", it carries `MemoryCare LLC`, the legal address (still a placeholder), the AMD amount and the reference the client types into their bank. It needs a template in the handoff package alongside the email template.

**G10. Cookie banner.** 04 forbids one covering the CTA; 03 refuses one in the portal; nobody says whether we need one. **Ruling: ship no non-essential cookies at launch.** 05 already self-hosts fonts; drop third-party analytics from the marketing site for v1 and use server-side page counts. Then there is no banner, no consent modal, and no fixed element competing with the `MobileActionBar` for the bottom 64px of a 640px screen. If the owner wants Google Analytics, that decision buys a banner, and the banner costs conversions at the exact moment the hero is doing its work.

**G11. `{REPORT_SLA}` and the callback window are load-bearing variables with no value.** 03 and 04 both write "within one working day" into production copy and both flag it. It appears in the hero-adjacent reassurance line, the form confirmation, the email, and the portal first-entry screen. A first promise we break is worse than a vaguer one. Owner decision, not ours.

**G12. Armenian and Russian plural forms.** 05 names it (ICU, Russian has three forms, Armenian two) and hands it to Igor; nobody wrote the affected strings. "N visits", "N monuments", "N m²", "N days left" appear in the calculator, the tariff cards, the credit countdown and the visit list. # 4. Factually wrong — arithmetic, impossible pairs, states that cannot exist

**F1. 02's opacity ladder is arithmetically false, and two of its rungs fail AA.** Measured against the official palette:

| 02's claim | Actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Anthracite 70% on Nude = "7.1:1, passes" | **4.26** | **fails 4.5** |
| Anthracite 60% on Nude = "6.3:1" (language switcher, inactive items) | **3.31** | **fails badly** |
| Ivory 80% on Anthracite = "7.9:1" | 7.39 | passes, number wrong |

**Correction:** secondary text on light is **#606161 (Anthracite at 76%), 4.99 on Nude, 5.34 on Ivory** — which is exactly 05's `text-secondary`, arrived at independently and correctly. 05's rule "there is no `text.tertiary`; anything below 4.5 is not a text token" is adopted system-wide, and 02's 45%/60%/70% rungs are deleted for text. The inactive language-switcher item takes `text-secondary`, not 60% Anthracite.

**F2. Two proposals specify a badge whose label cannot be read.**
- 03 §C.3: *"the badge is Olive **fill** with Anthracite label, which passes."* It does not: **Anthracite on Olive = 3.08.**
- 04 §A6 and §B4: *"an Olive band with Anthracite text carrying 'Most chosen'."* Same pair, same failure.

05 has this right and states the reason on the component: no label is legible on an Olive chip at any size (3.08 / 3.42). **"Most chosen" is Deep Olive fill with an Ivory label (6.01)** on light, and Nude label on the Anthracite band. This is also the brief's own rule — Olive never carries text — extended correctly to text *on* Olive, which the brief does not spell out and which is where both errors came from.

**F3. 04's above-the-fold list does not fit.** B1 requires, in ~640px at 360: header, H1, subhead, **the proof card visible not below the fold**, and the primary CTA. Header 64 + eyebrow 20 + H1 at three lines ≈ 120 + subhead at three lines ≈ 78 + CTA 48 + inter-block spacing ≈ 72 leaves roughly **238px** for a report artefact that needs ~300 to carry a 4:3 image plus a metadata row. **Resolution:** the CTA and the *top* of the ReportCard are above the fold; the card is deliberately cropped by the fold (my A3.1 rule), and a compact `ReportCard--hero` variant is specified at 360 — metadata row, GPS chip, one 16:9 crop at ~120px — instead of the full document. Both requirements survive; the full-fidelity card is what the visitor scrolls one thumb-length to reach.

**F4. Two pinned bottom bars.** Covered in C9 — as written, 02's calculator result bar and everyone's sticky CTA bar occupy the same fixed coordinates on the same route. It is a state that cannot exist.

**F5. 03 tells a subscriber their first paid visit is a survey.** §A.10.2: *"The first visit is the survey visit — the crew locates the plot, records the GPS point and photographs its current condition."* Read against §0 rule 7 ("every visit is a full visit") and the product table, that says an Optimal client's first of four full visits is a no-cleaning survey. Only **Inspection** is a survey. **Correction:** *"On the first visit the crew locates the plot and records its GPS point, and then does the full work — so every later report can be compared with the first."* Same reassurance, no contradiction, no visit silently consumed.

**F6. 03 §A.3 is internally inconsistent** — the heading "The three annual subscriptions" sits over four cards, one of which (Express) is a one-off that its own §0 rule 8 and §C.3 place elsewhere, and one of which (Special) has no price. Resolved by C1.

**F7. 05 contradicts its own token file.** "Nothing below 13px exists in the system at all" versus `mc.type.overline` at `0.75rem` (12px), and §E5 forbidding the tagline "at under 11px" (implying 11px is permitted). Resolved by C13.

**F8. 05's danger-button contrast figure is copied from the wrong row.** "bg `danger-600`, fg white (6.84)" — 6.84 is white on Deep Olive. White on `#8C3A2E` is **7.61**. Harmless direction, but the contrast table is the linter's source of truth and must be right.

**F9. 05's calculator count-up contradicts 05's own motion policy** ("Autoplay: None. Nothing on this site moves on its own") and 02's explicit prohibition. Resolved by C14.

**F10. A flow that dead-ends: the guest report's only exit is into the sales site.** 03's guest footer offers "About MemoryCare" — and every marketing page carries a sticky consultation CTA. One tap from a photograph of a grave to a sales bar is precisely the failure 03 §C.7 says it would defend hardest against. **Ruling: the guest report footer contains one sentence and a `tel:` link, and no navigation into the marketing site.** The single interactive element permitted on the guest view is a text link, *"Something wrong with this report? Tell us"* → three fields (name, phone, message) filing a guarantee re-visit against that visit and notifying the Owner. Support, never sales. 05's B6 (the guest route physically cannot import `TariffCard`, `Calculator`, `Badge--accent` or any `primary` button) is the right enforcement and is adopted — the escalation is a tertiary link, which that bundle still permits.

**F11. Nobody's cancellation flow can produce a number.** 03's cancellation copy, 04's "states the pro-rata amount as a number before the confirm step" and my four-step screen all assume an amount the system cannot compute until G1 and G3 are answered. Until then the screen has no success state.
