## 1. The terminology table

Nine concepts, three languages, one word each. **Variation for elegance is a
defect.** On a site whose whole proposition is that our statements are precise
enough to check, a synonym reads as a second thing.

The Armenian and Russian canonical forms below are **my proposals**. W-HY and
W-RU each get **one round** to contest a term in writing, with a reason that is
about meaning or grammar and not about taste. After that round the table is
frozen for the build and a change costs a lead ruling.

### 1.4 Two rulings the table depends on

**a. `այց`, not `այցելություն`.** This is the only place in this document where
I let a layout budget decide a word, and I want the reason on the record.
`այցելություն` is the natural Armenian noun and it is what the live site uses.
It is also twelve characters, and slots 27 (14 hard), 48 (20 hard), 49 (56 hard)
and 52 (44 hard) are all built around a two- or three-character English word.
`լիարժեք այցելություն տարեկան` is 28 characters against a 20-character hard
budget; `լիարժեք այց տարեկան` is 19. One of the two words fits the product and
the other does not. **W-HY may overturn this only by showing a grammatical
problem with `այց` in the specific constructions the slots need** — not by
preferring the longer word. If W-HY overturns it, four hard budgets move and
that is a lead ruling, not a writer's choice.

**b. `отчёт`, never `фотоотчёт`.** The Russian source documents
(`TARIFF-REDESIGN`, `PROJECT-MEMORY-FULL`) say `фотоотчёт` throughout, and W-RU
will inherit it without noticing, because it is the company's own internal word.
It is wrong on the site for one reason: the entire positioning rests on the
report being **photo and video and a GPS point**, and `фотоотчёт` names one
third of it. It also happens to be the exact word the incumbent's category uses.
The site says `отчёт`, and the composition is stated in the sentence next to it.

## 2. The banned-strings list

A hit fails the build. There is no warning level; a warning is a ban nobody

- **`40 000` with a space is the dangerous one**, because Russian and Armenian

  string also contains `Express`/`Էքսպրես`/`Экспресс`. Whitelist by key, never
  by "the reviewer will notice."

### 3.1 Three rulings before the table, because the brief contradicts itself

**Ruling A — `hard` beats `×1.30`, and the brief gets this wrong.** This
folder's `BRIEF.md` says "the English budget × 1.30 is the build target."
`PROPOSAL-ux.md` §10 says "where a budget is marked **hard**, the string must
not exceed it in **any** of the three languages and content chooses a shorter
formulation rather than the component growing." These are different
instructions and a writer following the first will blow every hard slot by 30%.

**The rule, and it is not negotiable:** on a **hard** slot the Armenian and
Russian budgets are **the same number as the English**, and the writer shortens.
`×1.30` is the **component's** build target on **soft** slots only — it tells
the engineer how much room to leave, not the writer how much to spend. In the
table below, hard slots show `= EN` and soft slots show the ×1.30 figure.

**Ruling B — every slot is three strings, and the register counts one.** Slot
53 is "4 feature lines × 3 cards" = twelve English strings = thirty-six strings
built. "123 slots" is a count of *slots*, not of work. The real string count
under this register is **roughly 640 per locale, about 1,920 in total**, plus
15 routes × 3 locales of meta title and description. Anyone planning this round
on "123 strings" has under-scoped it by a factor of five, and that arithmetic
is not in the brief.

**Ruling C — `֏` and digits do not count against a budget the way letters do,
and I am counting them anyway.** A character budget exists because of rendered
width, and `160,000 ֏ AMD` is 13 characters of tabular figures that occupy far
more width than 13 Armenian lowercase letters. The register counts characters
because that is what a person can check. Where a slot is mostly numerals
(52, 58, 67, 68), treat the budget as advisory and check the **rendered** width
in the narrowest container it appears in. Those rows are marked `†`.

**Ruling D — the deliverable is desktop web only (owner, 01.09), so a large
part of §10's budget table is now measuring a container we are not building.**
`PROPOSAL-ux.md` §8 is out of scope in full: no 360 breakpoint, no thumb reach,
no sticky CTA, no mobile drawer. **§10's budgets were not all set by the same
thing**, and the difference now matters:

| Budget origin | Effect of desktop-only |
|---|---|
| **A 360px container** — "two lines at 32px on 360", "must fit a 360 card", "three lines at 360" | **Re-derive.** These are measuring a screen we are not shipping |
| **A card, a chip or a column that exists at every width** | **Modest relief only.** A three-card row at 1200 gives each card roughly the width a full-bleed card had at 360. Do not assume desktop is generous inside a card |
| **A 900px+ container** — "fit one line at 900" | **Unchanged.** 900 is inside the desktop range |
| **Not a layout constraint at all** — meta titles (search-result truncation), email subjects (mail-client truncation), the weather paragraph (a content-order rule) | **Unchanged, and must not be loosened.** These have nothing to do with our grid |

**A budget that is tighter than the container is a tax on the writing for no
return**, and Armenian pays it three times over. §3.4 re-derives every slot in
the first row of that table. Everything in rows three and four keeps its number.

**Ruling E — two floors survive the scope cut and are not mobile rules.**
**Body text never below 16px; no informational text below 14px anywhere.** They
sat next to the mobile specifics in the source documents and they are not about
screen width — they are about a 40–60 audience reading the verification rail,
which carries the date, the cemetery, the plot, the crew and the coordinates.
That rail *is* the proof. Copy consequence: **no slot may be solved by setting
its string smaller.** If a re-derived budget still does not fit, the component
grows or the string changes; the type size is not available as an answer. The
uppercase chips (45, 47, 56) stay at 14px minimum, which is why their budgets
loosen less than their containers do.

### 3.5 The four structural answers I propose

**a. Slot 2, the primary button.** Do not translate `Request a consultation`.

every Kind-2 slot: one act, three native expressions of it.**

**b. Slot 13, meta titles.** 60 characters cannot hold category + city + brand

page exists. English and Russian keep the brand because English is where the
dementia-care collision lives and the brand suffix is the disambiguator. Three
different title *structures* is correct here; one structure applied three times
is the failure.

**c. Slots 16 and 18, the frozen promises.** They are frozen *as a commitment*,
not as a character string. **Ruling: freeze the commitment — one business day,
48 hours — and freeze the number's position in the sentence; let each language
find its own shortest true form, then freeze that form.** The three frozen
strings become nine frozen strings (three slots × three locales) and any
variation from the frozen nine is a defect exactly as before. What must never
happen is a locale softening `one business day` to `usually` or sharpening
`48 hours` to `24`.

**d. Slot 48 and the caption family.** Adopt `այց` (§1.4a). If W-HY overturns
it, slots 27, 48, 49 and 52 all move together as one ruling, not one at a time.

## 4. The style sheet

Binding in all three languages unless a rule says otherwise. Where a rule says
"per language", the per-language form is fixed here and not left to the writer.

### 4.1 Sentence length and rhythm

- **Body sentences: 12–18 words in English, target average 15.** Russian and
  Armenian will run longer in words and that is expected; the test is not word
  count but **one idea per sentence**.
- **Hard ceiling: no sentence over 28 words in any language.** A sentence a
  reader has to re-read on a phone at 01:00 has failed regardless of grammar.
- **Paragraphs: 2–4 sentences.** The honesty panel and the weather paragraph
  are the two permitted exceptions and both have their own budgets (36, 81).
- **First sentence of every block is declarative and contains a fact.** Not a
  set-up, not a question, not a mood. The register is fact-before-feeling, and
  the cheapest way to enforce it is a rule about position.
- **No sentence begins with `We believe`, `We understand`, `We know that`.**
  Those are three ways of writing nothing.
- **The polite form throughout, without exception:** Russian `Вы` / `Ваш`
  (capitalised in direct address), Armenian `Դուք` / `Ձեր`. Never `ты` / `դու`,
  not in an error message, not in a push notification, not in a chip. This
  audience is 35–60 and one `ты` costs more than a bad headline.

### 4.2 Capitalisation

- **Sentence case for every heading, every button, every label, every chip
  except where uppercase is a design token.** Never Title Case in English.
- **Uppercase is permitted only in:** the logo tagline as delivered, the unit
  chips (`ONE-OFF` / `PER YEAR`, slot 47), the `One-off · not a subscription`
  chip (45) and the recommendation badge (56) — all four at 14px minimum.
- **Armenian uppercase is avoided in badges and micro-labels.** Armenian
  all-caps at 14px is materially harder to read than Latin all-caps at the same
  size and it is not a convention Armenian readers expect in interfaces. Where
  a design token calls for uppercase, the Armenian string is set in normal case
  and the token is overridden for `hy`. This is a design instruction and I am
  putting it here because it is a copy consequence.
- **Product names are capitalised as proper nouns** in all three languages:
  `Optimal` · `Оптимал` · `Օպտիմալ`. Never `optimal`, never `ОПТИМАЛ`.
- **`MemoryCare`** — one word, two capitals, never wrapped across a line,
  never `Memory Care`, `MEMORYCARE` or `MC`. Legal entity `MemoryCare LLC`
  in English, and the registered Armenian/Russian forms of the entity name
  where a legal document requires them — W-LEG supplies those, nobody invents
  them.
- **No rhetorical questions as headings**, in any language. FAQ questions are
  questions the reader asks, which is a different thing and is permitted.

### 4.3 Numerals and currency

- **Digits, always, from zero up.** `4 full visits`, never `four full visits`.
  Never `four (4)`. The exception is a sentence-initial number, which is
  rewritten rather than spelled out.
- **Thousands separator: a comma, in all three languages, without exception.**
  `160,000`. **I am aware this is typographically wrong for Russian and
  Armenian**, both of which conventionally group with a thin space. I am ruling
  for the comma anyway, and here is the trade: a space-grouped `40 000` is
  invisible to a build-time check unless every check normalises four different
  Unicode space characters correctly on every surface, and the one price this
  site must never print again is a space-grouped one. One typographic
  compromise, applied consistently and visible to every reader as a deliberate
  house style, buys a check that actually fires. **If the lead overrules me,
  the §2.4 check must be re-specified first, not after.**
- **Currency format, no exceptions:** `160,000 ֏ AMD` — numeral, space, `֏`,
  space, `AMD`. In body copy, tables, the calculator, the portal, email, the
  invoice and the PDF. Never `֏160,000`, never `AMD 160,000`, never `160k`,
  never the symbol alone, never the letters alone.
- **`֏` is emitted in its own element with its own font stack**, in all three
  locales. Nobody has verified that Ghea Mariam or Montserrat Arm carries
  U+058F, and FINDINGS #21 shows the glyph already falling back to a system face
  on the live build and rendering visibly smaller than the digits beside it. A
  missing glyph must degrade for one character, not break a price.
- **Every non-AMD figure is marked approximate and never appears in a total.**
  Format: `≈ $410` / `≈ 410 $` per locale convention, always with the FX note
  string nearby. Charges are in AMD; that sentence appears wherever a foreign
  figure does. **This is bank condition 5 and it currently has no slot** (§3.6).
- **Ranges use an en dash with no spaces:** `20–40 seconds`, `09:00–18:00`,
  `35–60`. Never a hyphen, never `20 - 40`, never `from 20 to 40` in a label
  (that form is permitted in flowing body copy where it reads better).
- **A price range is never written.** There are five products and four fixed
  prices; a range implies negotiation, which is precisely the thing the
  calculator exists to deny. `from 20,000 ֏ AMD` is banned as a construction.
- **Measurements:** `16 m²`, non-breaking space between numeral and unit,
  superscript two as U+00B2, never `m2` or `кв.м` or `քմ`.
- **Percentages** do not appear on the site at all. There is nothing to express
  as a percentage except a discount, and there are no discounts.

### 4.4 Dates and times

- **Dates are written out, always:** `14 September 2026` · `14 сентября
  2026 г.` · `2026 թ. սեպտեմբերի 14`. **Never** `14/09/26`, `09/14/26`,
  `14.09.2026` or any all-numeric form — an American and a European reader read
  the same string as two different days, and half this audience is American.
  The all-numeric ban is a build check (§2.5).
- **Months are named, never numbered.** Each locale uses its own month names in
  its own script; no transliteration.
- **Times are 24-hour with a leading zero:** `09:00–18:00`. Never `9am`,
  never `9 утра`.
- **Every time-of-day commitment carries the offset:** `(UTC+4)`, in that
  form, in all three locales. Not `GMT+4`, not `Yerevan time` alone. Half the
  audience reads the page at 23:40 in Glendale and must be able to convert
  without searching.
- **A credit expiry is a plain date, never a countdown, never "X days left".**
  A timer on a memorial-care purchase is a pressure device.
- **Durations:** `within one business day` · `within 48 hours` — the two frozen
  promises and their frozen locale forms (§3.5c). `48 hours` never becomes
  `two days` and never becomes `1–2 days`.
- **Seasons are named, never dated.** `one in each season`. Never
  "every three months", never "quarterly", and never `monthly` in any language,
  in any context, ever.

### 4.5 Product names and the one-script rule

- **One script per locale, for words.** The English and Russian sites carry no
  Armenian words; the Armenian site carries no Latin product names. `֏` appears
  in all three because it is a currency sign, not copy.
- **This retires `FINAL-CONTENT` §4.2's rule** — "English name first, Armenian
  in parentheses on first mention" — which produced `Optimal (Օպտիմալ խնամք)` on
  the English page. That is Armenian words on the English site and it is now
  forbidden. Every string in `FINAL-CONTENT` §6.3 carrying a parenthetical
  Armenian name is dead copy (§5.2).
- **`GPS` stays Latin in all three locales**, as does `WhatsApp`, `Viber` and
  `MemoryCare`. Proper nouns and technical initialisms are not "Armenian words
  in the English locale" and the rule was never about them. Russian: `GPS`, not
  `ДжиПиЭс`. Armenian: `GPS`, not `ՋիՓիԷս`.
- **Product name forms per locale** — HY is the display name on the Armenian
  site; EN and RU names are **[OPEN]** and must actually be written this round
  (§6.7):

| | HY | RU | EN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inspection | `Զննում` (confirmed) | Осмотр | Inspection |
| Express | `Էքսպրես` **[OPEN]** — with or without `խնամք` | Экспресс | Express |
| Optimal | `Օպտիմալ` **[OPEN]** | Оптимал | Optimal |
| Maximum | `Մաքսիմում` **[OPEN]** | Максимум | Maximum |
| Special | `Հատուկ խնամք` **[OPEN]** | Особый уход | Special |

Whether `խնամք` stays in the Armenian names is an owner or localiser decision,
not a writer's. It changes slot 46 (22 hard) from comfortable to tight.

### 4.6 Phone numbers, for a reader who must dial internationally

This is a conversion surface, not a formatting preference. The live site prints
`+374 10-00-00-00` — a placeholder formatted to look real (FINDINGS #14) — and
that is the failure this rule exists to prevent.

- **Display form, identical in all three locales, left-to-right, never
  localised:** `+374 55 315 323` and `+374 93 154 108`. Country code with `+`,
  then space-separated groups. **No hyphens. No brackets. No `(0)`. No leading
  `0` trunk prefix.** A diaspora reader who dials a trunk `0` from abroad gets
  nothing.
- **`tel:` href is the unspaced E.164 form:** `tel:+37455315323`. The displayed
  and dialled forms differ and that is correct — never put the spaced form in
  the href.
- **`wa.me` links carry digits only, no `+`:** `https://wa.me/37455315323`.
- **Every published number is a live `tel:` link at every viewport**, and every
  number that takes WhatsApp is also a `wa.me` link. Both founders' numbers take
  WhatsApp and Viber; say so once, in the contact block, in one sentence.
- **Numbers are never split across a line break.** Wrap the label, never the
  number.
- **Armenian and Russian never reorder or re-punctuate a phone number**, and no
  locale ever writes it in Armenian or Cyrillic digits.
- **A number the user enters** is stored E.164 and displayed formatted for its
  own country; `+1 818 555 0142`, `(818) 555-0142` and `818.555.0142` are all
  accepted and normalised silently. Never block on paste.
- **Nothing that is not real is formatted to look real.** Until the legal
  address and registration number exist, they render as
  `[legal address — to be confirmed]`, visibly a gap. This applies to the
  ritual-row price (slot 71) and guarantee #2's liability figure (74) as well.

### 4.7 Punctuation

- **No exclamation marks.** Zero, in any language, in any state, including
  email and push. This is a build check.
- **No emoji.** Same scope.
- **Armenian punctuation is Armenian:** `։` as the full stop, `՝` and `,` as
  written, `՞` as the question mark placed on the stressed syllable — not `?`
  at the end. W-HY owns this and no build check second-guesses it. But the
  strings must not mix systems: an Armenian sentence ending in `.` is a defect.
- **The middot `·`** is the separator in verification strips, arithmetic lines
  and metadata rails, with a space on each side, in all three locales.
- **Em dash `—` with spaces** for parenthetical breaks in English and Russian;
  Armenian follows W-HY's convention. Never `--`.
- **`…` is one character** and appears only in `Loading…` and `Sending…`.
- **Quotation marks per locale:** English `“ ”`, Russian `« »`, Armenian `« »`.
  Never straight quotes anywhere.

### 4.8 Voice and construction

- **Active voice with a named actor.** Not "the visit was moved" — "we are
  moving the visit". Passive that hides who acted is a build-report warning
  (§2.5) and an editor rejection.
- **No hedges.** Either we commit or we say we do not know yet, in those words.
- **No scolding imperatives** — `Don't forget`, `Make sure you`, `Не забудьте`.
- **Bad news gets four things, in this order:** what happened, the proof, what
  happens next, and a named person with a phone number. That order is fixed
  across all three languages and all three bad-news states (slot 121).
- **Never put words in the dead person's mouth.** Not once, in any register, in
  any language.
- **The reader's absence is a fact, never a subject.** Distance and lack of time
  appear once, side by side, ranked equal, as circumstances.

### 4.9 The frozen strings

Nine strings (three slots × three locales) are frozen after the first round and
any variance is a defect: slot 16 (callback), 17 (hours qualifier) and 18
(report). They appear six times each. Nobody softens, sharpens or localises
them differently in a second place. The success state echoes slot 16 **verbatim**
(slot 109), it does not paraphrase it.

## 5. The truth register

Every factual claim the site makes, its source, and whether a stranger could
check it today. **A claim with no source is a defect and does not ship.**

Verdict key: **✅ checkable today** — a visitor or the bank could verify it now.
**⏳ true but not yet demonstrable** — true as a rule, no artefact to point at.
**🔴 not true today** — must not be written in the present tense.
**⛔ blocked** — no source; cannot be written at all.

### 5.1 Company and contact

### 6.3 The portal — the ruling nobody has made, and the one I am proposing

**The problem, stated exactly.** The site will describe a client portal and
Family Circle in the present tense. The platform's target readiness is
~20.09.2026, by an outside contractor, with no signed development contract
(`PROJECT-MEMORY-FULL` §9: "Договор с разработчиками — **не подписан**"). The

Meanwhile `FINAL-CONTENT` §4.4 froze the report promise as
**"The report is in your portal within 48 hours of the visit."** — the word
`portal` is inside the frozen string, in six places, in three languages. And
`PROPOSAL-ux` slot 18 froze a *different* string,
**"Your report arrives within 48 hours of the visit."** — which does not name a
channel at all.

**Two prior documents froze two different sentences for the same promise, and
one of them promises a screen that will not exist.** Nobody noticed because
nobody read them side by side. That is my job and here it is.

**Why this is the first promise a real customer meets.** A pilot client signs in
September, pays by bank transfer, receives a visit, and then goes looking for
the thing the site told them they would get. If what they find is a login page
that does not work, we have broken the one promise the entire brand is built on
— on a site whose proposition is *our promises are checkable* — at the first
possible opportunity, in front of the five to ten people whose experience is our
only real evidence for the next twelve months.

**My proposed ruling, and I want it in writing from Hayk before anyone writes
slot 18:**

1. **The frozen report promise names no channel.** `PROPOSAL-ux` slot 18 wins:
   *"Your report arrives within 48 hours of the visit."* It is true whether the
   report arrives as a portal notification or as a plain link in WhatsApp.
   **`FINAL-CONTENT` §4.4's portal wording is retired** (§5.2 below).
2. **Delivery is described by its mechanism, in the present tense, because the
   mechanism is real:** *"Each report arrives as a link you can open and
   forward. Nobody you send it to needs an account."* That is the owner's own
   decision of 26.08 §3.3, it is deliverable with an email and a URL on day one,
   and it is a better sentence than "log in to your portal" for a diaspora
   family group chat anyway.
3. **The portal is described in the future or the conditional until it is live,
   and never with a screenshot of a screen that does not exist.** Where a
   subscription's contents are listed, the portal appears as *what a
   subscription includes* — which is true, it is a term of the sale — not as
   *what you can go and look at now*.
4. **Family Circle, identically.** It is *what a subscription includes*. The
   Family Circle page describes the model, the four roles and the privacy rules
   — all of which are decided and true — and does not show or imply a live
   screen.
5. **`coming soon` is banned in all three languages** (§2.5). The choice is
   between a true present tense about links and a true future tense about the
   portal. It is never a promise with a date on it.
6. **A single dated review point:** if the platform is live and stable, one
   ruling flips the tense across the site in one commit, because the portal
   sentences are isolated to a named set of keys. **W-LEG and the build engineer
   agree that key set before writing**, so the flip is a data change and not a

If the lead overrules me and ships the portal in the present tense, I want the
overruling on the record with a name against it, because the cost lands on a

## 7. The acceptance test

Run by one person, on the three copy decks plus the legal deck, in this order.
**Gate 0 is disqualifying: a deck that fails it is returned unread.** Everything
after gate 0 is scored and the failures are listed, not argued about.

Budget: about four hours for all four decks, by one editor, on a laptop, with
the string files open. It is designed to be applicable without any tooling
except the checks in §2.6.

---

### Gate 0 — Truth. Binary. Disqualifying.

Read every string in the deck against §5. Ask one question of each:
**can a stranger check this today, and does the tense match its verdict?**

The deck is returned unread if it contains **any** of:

- a number of customers, families, graves, services, reviews, stars or years
- a testimonial, a name attached to a quote, or a partner logo slot
- `the only` · `the first` · `nobody else` · `unique` · `since 20xx`, in any
  language
- any competitor named or alluded to, including inside an FAQ answer
- `light visit` · `heavy visit` · `preventive visit`, or any translation
- `40,000` or `45,000` as an Express price; `60,000`, `80,000`, `180,000`,
  `240,000` anywhere
- `monthly` · `bestseller` · `most chosen` · `most popular`, in any language
- a QR code, a memory page or a digital memorial, in any form, including
  "coming later"
- a guarantee stated without the figure that binds it (guarantee #2)
- **the portal or Family Circle described in the present tense as something the
  reader can go and look at** (§6.3)
- a promise of a date for card payment
- a claim with no entry in §5

**One hit fails the gate.** There is no partial credit and no "we'll fix it in
review" — this gate exists because the last build shipped 150,000 invented
customers and three fabricated testimonials illustrated with photographs of real
people, and everyone involved was sure it would be caught later.

Run §2.6 surface H (the automated check, on the markdown decks) **before**
reading anything. It takes a minute and it catches perhaps 70% of gate-0
failures without human attention. Then read, because it catches the other 30%.

---

### Gate 1 — Terminology. Binary.

For each of the nine concepts in §1.1 and the nine in §1.3, grep the deck for
the canonical term and for every banned synonym. **Any banned synonym in any
locale fails the gate**; the fix is mechanical and takes minutes, so this is a
return-and-resubmit, not a rewrite.

Then, the check a grep cannot do: **is the same concept named by the same word
in every one of its occurrences?** Pick three concepts at random and read every
occurrence of each. A writer who used two words for the visit in a single deck
has not understood the round.

---

### Gate 2 — The frozen strings.

Nine strings (slots 16, 17, 18 × three locales). Find every occurrence — six
each, expected 54 total. **Every occurrence byte-identical to its frozen form.**
Not "the same meaning". Identical.

Then check slot 109: the success state must echo slot 16 verbatim, not a
paraphrase of it.

---

### Gate 3 — Budgets, measured.

For every slot marked **hard** in §3.3, paste the string into a character
counter and compare to the English budget. Not `×1.30`. The English budget.

Produce two lists:
- **Over budget and shortenable** → returned to the writer.
- **Over budget and not shortenable without lying or breaking grammar** → goes
  to the lead as a **Kind-1 budget change** (§3.4), with the writer's one-line
  reason. This list is expected to be long in Armenian and that is the system
  working, not failing.

For soft slots, check against the ×1.30 figure and flag anything over 1.5× —
that is a writer who did not know a budget existed.

**Do not skip this gate because it is boring.** It is the gate that decides
whether QA finds twenty-eight broken components in October.

---

### Gate 4 — The translation tell.

The brief's stated most-likely failure. Applicable by a person in twenty
minutes:

1. Take the five longest body strings in each locale.
2. **Count the clauses and count the sentences.** Three locales whose strings
   have the same clause count and the same sentence boundaries in the same order
   are three translations of one text. Native writing diverges structurally —
   Armenian will fold two English sentences into one and split another into
   three.
3. **Read the Armenian aloud to someone who has not read the English.** Ask them
   what the company does. If they answer in the site's own vocabulary, it works.
   If they hesitate over a phrase, that phrase is calqued.
4. **Check the pre-committed five.** Each writer submitted their five hardest
   slots before the deck (§6.2). Compare them to the deck's final versions: if
   the deck's versions have converged on identical structure, someone
   back-translated.

This gate produces a judgement, not a pass/fail. A deck that fails it goes back
for the affected sections, not for a rewrite.

---

### Gate 5 — The register.

Sample twenty strings per locale at random and apply §4:

- Any exclamation mark, any emoji → fail (should already be caught).
- Any all-numeric date → fail.
- Any rhetorical question in a heading, chip or button → fail.
- Any `ты` / `դու` → fail.
- Any sentence over 28 words → flag.
- Any block whose **first sentence** is a set-up rather than a fact → flag.
- Any price not in the exact form `160,000 ֏ AMD` → fail.
- Any phone number with hyphens, brackets, a trunk zero, or a `tel:` href
  carrying spaces → fail.
- Any non-AMD figure without an approximation marker → fail.

---

### Gate 6 — Completeness against the register.

Walk §3.3 top to bottom. For each of the 123 slots, in each locale:
**present, `[BLOCKED — reason]`, or missing.** Missing is a defect; blocked with
a reason is acceptable and goes on the blockers list.

Then check the six unslotted-but-mandated items in §3.6. If nobody wrote the
logo descriptor or the FX note, they are not going to appear later by themselves.

Expected blockers at this point, all legitimate: T-2 (registration number),
T-7 (business hours), T-27 / slot 71 (ritual price), T-49 / slot 74 #2
(liability figure), and slot 46 in Armenian if the product names are still
unconfirmed.

---

### Gate 7 — The bank package.

W-LEG's deck only, and it is a checklist, not a judgement. Eight conditions:

1. About page — exists, in three locales, with the company, the people and the
   legal entity.
2. Real contacts in the footer of **every** page — both numbers, the email, the
   address, the registration number.
3. Full service descriptions — all **five** products with exact visit counts.
4. Legal restrictions — `/legal/limitations/`, including the municipal-permission
   limit on construction work.
5. Real AMD prices **with an FX note** — the note is the half that is missing
   today.
6. Privacy policy **in English**.
7. Refund policy, with the pro-rata arithmetic shown.
8. Service-delivery terms — when the visit happens after payment.

Any condition not covered is a delay to card revenue, not a copy note.

---

### Gate 8 — The two reader tests, in this order.

**a. The ninety-second stranger.** Give the home-page deck, on a phone, to
someone who knows nothing about this company. Ninety seconds. Then ask, without
letting them look back: *What does this company do? Where? What does it cost?
What will you get after they visit? What do you do next?* Five answers, five
facts. Any answer they cannot give is a hierarchy failure in that locale, and
the three locales must be tested separately — they will not fail in the same
places.

**b. Davit's test, and it is the tie-break.** Would Davit send this page,
unaccompanied, to a stranger whose mother died in March? Read it as that
stranger. If any sentence makes you flinch, name it. That flinch is the
brand's actual specification and it is worth more than any of the gates
above it.

---

### What the editor delivers back

One file per deck, containing exactly four lists and nothing else:

1. **Gate-0 hits** — if non-empty, the deck is returned and lists 2–4 are not
   written.
2. **Defects** — slot ID, locale, what is wrong, which rule.
3. **Budget changes requested** — slot ID, locale, measured length, budget, the
   writer's one-line reason. Goes to the lead as one batch, not as twenty
   conversations.
4. **Blockers** — slot ID, what is missing, whose name it needs.

No prose, no praise, no discussion. The disagreement is in this document; the
review is arithmetic.
