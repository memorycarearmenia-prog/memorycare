## 1. Positioning

### The one sentence the site must make a visitor believe

> **This company will show me, in evidence I can check and forward to my
> family, exactly what was done at our grave and when — on a schedule I do
> not have to manage.**

Read what that sentence does *not* contain. No adjective. No feeling. No
distance. No "peace of mind". The verb is **show**, the object is **evidence**,
and the qualifier is **checkable**. Everything else on the page is in service
of that sentence or it is decoration.

Why this sentence and not the obvious one ("we care for your family's grave"):
the care is not the scarce thing. Anyone in Yerevan with a brush and a car can
cut grass. hush.am has been selling four visits a year with photographs since
about 2015; Dambaran does photo *and* video; there are private operators on
list.am. What is scarce — verifiably, across every market surveyed in the
research pass and specifically in Yerevan, where the head of one cemetery was
arrested for bribery in 2023 and Hetq has documented resold plots and stolen
flowers — is **proof that anyone actually stood at that grave and did the
work**. We are not selling grave care. We are selling an evidentiary standard
that happens to include grave care.

That reframe is also the only one that survives the price. 160,000 ֏ is roughly
the top of the American annual range and a large multiple of the local norm.
Nobody pays a premium for grass. People do pay a premium for not having to
wonder.

**The corrected pricing makes this reframe more important, not less.** Under
the superseded table I could argue visit intensity — six and nine visits
against a local standard of four. Under the live table Optimal is four full
visits, which is *the same count the incumbent advertises*. The volume
differentiator is gone. What remains is the quality of each visit and the
quality of the evidence, which is precisely what the sentence above sells. See
§2, defensible claim 3, which I have had to rewrite.

### The three proof pillars

**Pillar 1 — The visit protocol, published.**
A visit cannot be closed without a GPS point taken on site, eight photographs
(four angles before, the same four angles after) and two videos of 20–40
seconds (`PROJECT-MEMORY-FULL.md` §8). That is a written operational standard
and it is unusual. **Publish it as a spec, with the numbers.** Not "detailed
photo reports" — *"Eight photographs. Four angles before, the same four after.
Two videos. One GPS point, recorded at the plot. A visit is not closed until
all of it exists."* A competitor can copy the sentence in an afternoon; they
cannot copy having built the field workflow that enforces it, and if they copy
the sentence they are now arguing on our vocabulary. This is the pillar that
does the most work and it costs us nothing but the decision to be specific.

**Pillar 2 — Family Circle, framed as a structural fact, not a feature.**
An Armenian family plot has heirs, not a customer. One person pays; four people
worry. Every analog in the world treats the buyer as an individual account —
Tending's shared app access is the closest thing anyone has, and hush.am has
nothing. The line that sells it is not a feature list, it is an observation the
reader recognises: *care is rarely one person's decision and it should not be
one person's inbox.* This also quietly solves the two-audience problem (§4):
the aunt in Yerevan and the son in Glendale are in the same circle, looking at
the same report, and neither of them is addressed as a persona.

**Pillar 3 — Everything a stranger would want to check, published before they
ask.** Prices in AMD, with what is included. The five products with exact visit
counts. Guarantees with a cost attached to us. Real names, real phone numbers,
a real legal address and a registration number. The admission that we have no
clients yet. This pillar is the cheapest and it is the one that converts a
45-year-old in Glendale at one in the morning, because it is the only pillar
that behaves like a company with nothing to hide.

### The one thing we must never say

> **Any variant of "we are the only ones", "the first", or "nobody else in
> Yerevan does this."**

It is false, it is falsifiable in a single search, and the person most likely
to run that search is our best prospect — the diaspora buyer doing due
diligence before wiring money to a country they do not live in. One
contradicted claim on a page whose entire proposition is *trust the evidence*
destroys the proposition. hush.am has existed since ~2015, has an Android app,
a Facebook page with ~72 reviews at 94% recommend, GPS grave locating,
before/after photo reports and a Los Angeles phone number. If a visitor finds
that after reading "the only service of its kind in Armenia", we have not lost
a sale, we have proved we lie.

Banned as strings in all three languages, extending the prior art's §3.6:
`the only` · `the first` · `nobody else` · `unlike others` · `no one in
Yerevan` · `unique` · `revolutionary` · `since 20xx`. And: **no competitor is
ever named on the site**, in any language, including in an FAQ answer.

## 2. The competitive frame

### What is not defensible — kill these on sight

| Claim | Why it dies |
|---|---|
| "The only grave care with photo reports in Yerevan" | hush.am, Dambaran.am, Service Memorial. Checkable in one search. |
| "Photo reports" as the differentiator | The photo report is the category's table stakes worldwide — Tending, GraveCareUkraine, Grabpflegeservice24, BloomBridge, hush.am all lead with it. Saying it as a differentiator marks us as the newest entrant, not the best. |
| "GPS" unqualified | hush.am sells GPS *grave locating*. If we say "GPS" without saying what for, a visitor who has seen their site reads us as a copy. |
| "Video reports" as a differentiator | Dambaran.am already advertises photo **and** video. It is a component of our standard, not our headline. |
| **"More visits than anyone else"** | **Newly indefensible under the corrected table.** Optimal is four visits a year; the incumbent's advertised package is four visits a year. Any volume claim now invites the exact comparison we lose. |
| "We have our own field teams" | True, useful internally, unverifiable by a visitor, and worth nothing on a first screen. |
| "Premium" as an assertion | The word does no work. Price does the work, and price needs an argument (§6k). |
| "Years of experience", "trusted by N families", any counter | Zero customers. This is exactly what the current build ships and the reason we are rebuilding it. |

### What is defensible — and how to phrase it

1. **The evidence standard, quantified.** "Eight photographs, four angles
   before and the same four after, two videos, one GPS point taken at the plot;
   the visit does not close until all of it exists." No surveyed competitor
   publishes a protocol. Defensible because it is our own operating rule, it is
   specific, and it is checkable against the report the client receives.
2. **GPS as *verification*, not as *location*.** hush's GPS answers "where is
   the grave?". Ours answers "was the crew standing at it?". Phrase it that way
   every time — *the GPS point is recorded on site, at the plot, on the day* —
   and the two things stop being confusable.
3. **The depth of a single visit — this replaces the visit-count argument I
   made in the first draft, which the corrected pricing killed.** Optimal is
   four visits and so is the incumbent's package. So the claim can no longer be
   *how often*; it must be *what a visit is*. The corrected line-up helps here,
   because the light/heavy split is rejected: **every visit is a full visit —
   the whole plot and every monument, cleaned, not a look around.** That is a
   sentence the incumbent cannot match without publishing their own definition
   of a visit, which nobody in this category does. State the count plainly, put
   the weight on the definition, and never compare.
4. **The portal and the family sub-accounts.** No player found in any market
   combines photo + video + GPS + client portal + family circle. This is the
   one "no one combines all five" claim that survived the research pass — but
   it must be said as *what we do*, never as *what others don't*. Write "one
   plot, one family, separate accounts, every report visible to all of them."
   Do not write "unlike other services".
5. **Public prices in AMD.** Dambaran hides prices; hush's package price is not
   publicly indexed. Across Europe a transparent price list is the single
   biggest conversion lever in this category. Publishing five prices — four
   fixed and one calculated in the open — is a competitive act, not just a bank
   requirement. **A calculator that shows its arithmetic is a stronger version
   of this claim than a flat price was**, provided it computes in front of the
   reader and never asks for an email to reveal a number.
6. **Written guarantees with a cost to us.** Free repeat visit within seven
   days; we pay for damage we cause; pro-rata refund on cancellation. Only
   publish each of these once legal and insurance have actually bound them —
   `PROJECT-MEMORY-FULL.md` §9 still lists liability and worker insurance as
   open as of the archive. A guarantee we cannot honour is worse than no
   guarantee.

### The sceptical visitor who has already found hush.am

Assume they exist, because in Glendale they do. They arrive with one question:
*why you and not the ten-year-old one with the app?* We answer it without ever
mentioning them, in three places:

- **First screen:** something checkable and specific, not a mood — the report's
  metadata (date, cemetery, crew, GPS confirmed) rendered as an artifact.
  The comparison a visitor makes between two tabs is *spec sheet vs. promise*,
  and we should be the spec sheet.
- **A protocol section, one scroll down**, with the 8/4-4/2/GPS numbers.
- **One FAQ entry, neutrally worded: "How should I compare grave-care
  services?"** — a checklist of the questions worth asking (What exactly is
  done on one visit? What arrives after it? Can the rest of the family see it?
  Is the price published? What happens if the monument is damaged?). We satisfy
  every item; we name nobody. **This is the most contestable thing I am
  proposing** — it is one step from a comparison page, and a comparison page is
  where honest companies start lying. My argument for it: the visitor is going
  to run that comparison whether or not we help, and being the party that hands
  them the criteria is both an act of good faith and the strongest possible
  position when we happen to be the only one that clears them all. My condition
  for it: every item must be a question a reasonable buyer would ask
  independently, none may be reverse-engineered from a competitor's weakness,
  and if the copy lead cannot write it without a sneer, we cut the section.
  Note that under the corrected pricing the first item on that checklist must
  be *what is done on one visit*, not *how many visits* — the old ordering
  would have pointed at a tie.

The hardest problem on the site, and the one the current build "solved" by
inventing 150,000 customers, 15 years and three testimonials illustrated with
photographs of real public figures. Every legitimate device available to us,
ranked by strength.

| # | Device | Strength | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A real, complete, openable sample report for a real plot** — the actual artifact, at a stable URL, not a picture of one | Highest. It is the product; it converts by being examined rather than believed | High: needs the September shoot and a rendered report template | **Build it.** Nothing else on this list is close. |
| 2 | **The published visit protocol** (8 photos / 4 angles before + 4 after / 2 videos / GPS / visit cannot close without it) | Very high — specific, unusual, and checkable against #1 | Near zero; it already exists as an internal rule | **Build it.** Best ratio on the page. |
| 3 | **Named humans with real, dialable numbers and photographs** — Davit Hambardzumyan, +374 55 315 323; Hayk Manukyan, +374 93 154 108 | Very high for a cross-border buyer. A phone number that answers is the oldest trust device there is | Low: real contacts exist since 11.08 | **Build it.** Photograph them properly; do not use avatars. |
| 4 | **Written guarantees with a cost attached to us** (7-day redo, damage, pro-rata refund) | High — the only trust a pre-launch company can actually *buy* | Medium: requires legal + insurance actually bound | Build **once real.** Do not publish ahead of the policy. |
| 5 | **Published prices in AMD, with what is and is not included, and a calculator that shows its arithmetic** | High. | 6 | **The honesty statement** — "we started in 2026, we have no reviews to show you, and we will not borrow anyone else's" | High, and structurally uncopyable: an incumbent cannot say it | Zero | **Build it,** at body size in a bordered panel — never as small print. |

| 11 | **Insurance and the incident reserve** | Medium, high with local buyers | Gated on §9 open items | **Only when bound.** |
| 12 | **Pilot transparency** — publishing what the September pilot did, as it happens | Medium and rising; it converts our biggest weakness (no history) into a live feed of evidence | Medium ongoing | **Build the slot now**, fill it in October. |

testimonial, review count, star rating, customer number, years-in-business,
"as featured in", partner logos, or a partners carousel. The partners section
in the current build is four empty tiles under a heading (FINDINGS #22); it
should not be restyled, it should be deleted and the pattern removed from the
component library so it cannot come back.

**The one that is worth real money and real work:** #1. The entire strategy
rests on an artifact we do not yet have. See §6c — this is the largest risk in
the project and the brief does not mention it.

## 4. Two audiences, one page

### Where the needs genuinely diverge

| | Diaspora (US, RU, EU; 35–60) | Local premium (Yerevan; 40–60) |
|---|---|---|
| Real question | "Can I trust a remote transaction with a company I cannot visit?" | "Are these people competent, and will they turn up?" |
| Blocking unknown | Often *does not know where the plot is* — Yerevan cemeteries are documented as unmapped and hard to navigate | Knows exactly where it is; the problem is Tuesday |
| Payment | Foreign-issued card, currency confusion, cross-border wire anxiety | Local card or transfer; wants a receipt and a contract |
| Verification | The report **is** the visit. It is all they will ever have | Can and might drive over to check. Which is good for us — say so |
| Family | Distributed across timezones; the report gets forwarded | Local, sometimes in disagreement about who pays |
| Timing | Anniversaries, Easter, a trip planned in a year | **Merelots** — the day after each major feast, and a *working day*, which is precisely why they cannot go |
| Emotional register | Guilt, already well defended against; needs a route to relief, not a reminder | Time pressure, no guilt; responds to competence |

### How one page serves both

The convergence is total at the level of the proposition — both are buying
*delegation plus evidence* — and total at the level of the product. It diverges
only in **which facts each reader needs**. So: **one voice, one hierarchy, and
segment-specific facts placed where a reader self-selects by their own
question, never by a persona label.**

Concretely:

1. **The hero names neither audience and neither reason.** It states the
   outcome and shows the proof. Any hero that says "wherever you are" has
   already chosen the diaspora and told the local reader this is not for them.
2. **The two reasons appear once, side by side, in a single sentence in the
   body, ranked equal**: distance and time, named as circumstances, never as
   failings. Prior art's rule holds and I would strengthen it: *the reader's
   absence is a fact, never a subject.*
3. **The Inspection tariff (20,000 ֏) is the shared door.** For the diaspora it
   answers "I don't even know what condition it is in" — it is the paid,
   guaranteed version of the unfulfilled Find a Grave photo request, which is a
   documented behaviour with open unmet requests on Yerevan cemeteries today.
   For the local it is a low-commitment test of whether we are any good. One
   product, two motives, zero persona copy. This is the most under-used asset
   in the line-up and I would give it more prominence than "visually apart from
   the subscriptions" implies — it should read as *the way to start*. The
   60-day, one-credit-only rule makes this argument stronger than it was under
   a 30-day window: the reader can take the cheap first step without a deadline
   pressing on them.
4. **The FAQ carries the divergence.** A reader self-identifies by clicking a
   question, which costs them nothing and labels nobody: *"I am not sure exactly
   where the plot is." · "Can I come with the crew?" · "Can I pay with a card
   issued outside Armenia?" · "Can my brother see the reports without paying?"
   · "Do you visit before Merelots?"* Two of those five are diaspora questions,
   two are local, one is both. Nobody notices the design.
5. **Merelots is the one piece of content that serves both without
   segmenting** — it is a shared calendar, the local reader cannot attend
   because it falls on a working day, and the diaspora reader cannot attend
   because of geography. Same fact, same sentence, two reliefs.
6. **Language selection carries most of the segmentation for free** and must
   therefore be unmissable — which the current build gets exactly backwards,
   with 22.5px-tall language links that vanish entirely when the mobile menu
   opens (FINDINGS #25, #27).

**What we do not do:** no diaspora/local toggle, no tabs, no flags, no country
selector, no "for Armenians abroad" section, no map of the world with lines
converging on Yerevan, and no photograph of a person looking wistfully at a
phone. Any of these turns one brand into two brochures.

## 5. The naming and SEO problem

The finding: in English, "memory care" is the established industry term for
dementia care. Searches for the bare brand return American memory-care
facilities and Alzheimer's Care Armenia. Indexed mentions of MemoryCare /
memorycare.am: **zero, in all three languages.** Separately, the lawyer's note:
a descriptive English phrase registers badly as a word mark abroad.

This is not only an SEO problem. It is a **comprehension** problem, and it is
worse in the two seconds before the reader scrolls than it is in the SERP.

### The rules

1. **Never optimise for the bare brand phrase.** Traffic from "memory care" is
   the wrong traffic, arrives on the wrong intent, and bounces. Do not target
   it, do not buy it, do not write it as a page's subject.
2. **The brand never appears alone in a `<title>`.** Category first, brand
   last, in every locale:
   - EN: `Grave care in Yerevan — visit reports with photo, video and GPS | MemoryCare`
   - RU: `Уход за могилой в Ереване — отчёт с фото, видео и GPS | MemoryCare`
   - HY: `Գերեզմանի խնամք Երևանում — լուսանկար, տեսանյութ և GPS հաշվետվություն | MemoryCare`
   Keep each under 60 characters in the shipped versions; the above are the
   full forms to trim from, and the trim keeps the category, never the brand.
3. **Every `h1` contains the category words.** English: "grave", "Yerevan".
   Not "Honoring memory" — the slogan is a slogan, not a heading. A page whose
   `h1` does not say what the company does is unindexable and unreadable at the
   same time.
4. **A visible descriptor, adjacent to the logo, on every page, in the header.**
   English: **`Grave care in Yerevan`**. This is a design instruction, not a
   metadata one — the lock-up needs a descriptor slot and the visual lead must
   plan for it. It is our cheapest defence against a reader thinking, for two
   seconds, that we run a dementia home. Two seconds is the whole budget.
5. **Schema:** `Organization` + `LocalBusiness`. **Never `MedicalBusiness`,
   `MedicalOrganization` or any health-adjacent type** — that is the collision,
   encoded. Add `Service` with `areaServed: Yerevan`, `Offer` nodes with
   `priceCurrency: "AMD"` and the exact prices for the four fixed products,
   `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`, and an explicit `disambiguatingDescription`
   saying we are a cemetery-plot care service and not a dementia-care provider.
   The fifth product, Special, is priced by calculator and therefore carries an
   `Offer` with a `priceSpecification` describing how it is computed, or no
   `Offer` at all — **never a fabricated number to fill the field.** On the
   Armenian page only, `alternateName: "Մեմորի Քեր"`.
6. **Locale hygiene, from FINDINGS:** `hy`, not `am` — `am` is Amharic, and we
   are currently telling every crawler that our Armenian pages are Ethiopian.
   `<html lang>` correct per locale. Reciprocal `hreflang` for hy/ru/en plus
   `x-default`. A self-referencing canonical per locale. `/` redirects rather
   than serving a second copy of the Armenian home page.
7. **Compound and categorical queries only.** "grave care Yerevan", "cemetery
   care Armenia", "уход за могилой Ереван" (the research pass found this one
   essentially uncontested), "գերեզմանի խնամք Երևան", "как найти могилу
   родственника в Ереване" (currently routed to a competitor's FAQ — an open
   door).
8. **Word choice on the page:** the words *grave*, *cemetery*, *plot*,
   *monument* and *Yerevan* must appear above the fold in English. The
   temptation of the tone brief — restrained, editorial, euphemistic — points
   the opposite way, and the euphemism is what makes us invisible. Say
   "grave". The prior art's stop-list already bans the euphemisms
   (`resting place`, `the departed`); this is the same rule arriving from the
   SEO side.

**Out of this brief's scope but the highest-ROI marketing work available**, and
I am flagging it because nobody else will: per-cemetery landing pages
(Tokhmakh, Zeytun, Nor Norq, Nubarashen). Tending does it by state, Styks does
it by cemetery, both work, and the Armenian versions do not exist. Anyone
building the page templates should build them so this pattern costs a
content file, not a redesign.

## 6. Adversarial review

### a. The brief rebrands a site that still cannot take money — its largest omission

**This is the finding I most want acted on, and the only one in this round that
touches revenue timing.** Stated in full.

The route list in the brief is `home`, `contact`, `login`, `register`, `reset`,
`history`, `mission`, `values`, `news`, `notfound`, `account-index`,
`packages-add-1`, `root`.

Ameriabank's eight conditions for enabling internet acquiring
(`PROJECT-MEMORY-FULL.md` §8, screenshot at
`03-Документы_августа/tz/bank-requirements.png`) are:

| # | Bank condition | Route needed | In the brief's list? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An **About** page | `/about/` | **No** |
| 2 | **Real contacts in the footer of every page** | global footer | Partly — the footer exists, but FINDINGS #14 shows it carries `0000, Yerevan`, `+374 10-00-00-00` and no registration number |
| 3 | **Full service descriptions — every tariff with its exact visit count** | a tariffs / services page | **No** — the home page has a pricing section, but there is no route that describes the products in full, and there are now **five** products, one of them calculator-priced |
| 4 | **Legal restrictions** — what we will not or cannot do | `/legal/limitations/` | **No** |
| 5 | **Real prices in drams with an FX note** | tariffs page + home | Partly — prices yes, the FX note is nowhere in the brief |
| 6 | **Privacy policy, in English** | `/legal/privacy/` | **No** |
| 7 | **Refund / return policy** | `/legal/refund/` | **No** |
| 8 | **Service-delivery terms** — when the visit happens after payment | `/legal/terms/` | **No** |

**Six of the eight require pages that are not in the brief's route list at all,
and two more are only half-covered.**

The critical path recorded in `PROJECT-MEMORY-FULL.md` §8 is: **site ready →
submitted to the bank for review → bank enables acquiring → online card
payment**, with expected enablement **early October 2026**. The September pilot
runs *without* online payment, on bank transfer against a short contract. §8
also records the acceptance criterion for the whole platform: *a client in the
USA leaves a request and can pay by card, and sees the report in their
account.*

So: **if we deliver a beautiful rebrand of exactly the routes the brief lists,
the site cannot be submitted to the bank, and this rebrand sits on the critical
path of the company's first card revenue.** The brief mentions card payment
once, in a single clause, describing it as "secondary". It never mentions the
bank. Four specialists are currently designing against a route list that
guarantees a second round of work before the site can be submitted.

There is a second-order timing risk that nobody has written down: §8 also notes
that if the bank has not finished its review by the pilot, the pilot either
slips or takes money by transfer only. Every week this rebrand spends not
covering the eight conditions is a week added to that review, because the bank
cannot start until the pages exist.

**Ruling needed, and it is a scope change, not a preference.** The deliverable
must include, as designed routes with the same brand treatment as the home
page:

- `/about/` — About, carrying the company, the people, the legal entity, the
  registration number and the honesty statement.
- A **tariffs / services** route carrying all five products in full, each with
  its exact visit count and what a visit is, plus the calculator for Special.
- `/legal/privacy/` (English mandatory, all three locales ideally),
  `/legal/refund/`, `/legal/terms/` (service-delivery timing), and
  `/legal/limitations/`.
- A **global footer** with the real address, the real phones, `info@memorycare.am`
  and the company registration number, identical on every page and every locale.
- An **FX note** wherever a non-AMD figure appears, marking it approximate and
  stating that the charge is in AMD.

And the converse: **`history`, `mission`, `values` and `news` should be deleted
from the navigation, not designed.** All four currently render the 404 template
under HTTP 200 (FINDINGS #4). `mission` and `values` are corporate filler for a
company with no customers; `news` is a content commitment nobody has staffed
and an empty newsroom is a negative trust signal. Fold whatever matters into
About. This is not extra scope — it is four routes of design work traded for
six routes the bank actually requires, and it is close to cost-neutral.

### b. The pricing facts contradicted themselves across three documents — now resolved, and my conclusion was partly wrong

**Original finding, kept for the record:** the brief, `CLAUDE.md` and
`FINAL-UX`/`FINAL-CONTENT` disagreed on the line-up, the visit counts, the
credit windows, the existence of a fifth product, the existence of surcharges
and the existence of a calculator; and the brief re-stated a repeat-Express
price of 40,000 ֏ that FINDINGS #3 records as cancelled on 25.08.2026. My
recommendation was that the design lead should declare the brief's table
authoritative and rule the fifth product, the surcharges and the calculator
dead.

**That recommendation was wrong, and in the useful direction.** The owner
superseded the whole table on 26.08.2026 and `CLAUDE.md` had never been
updated, so the brief was carrying a stale line-up while I was arguing it
should be treated as the source of truth. The prior art I was warning people
away from was closer to correct than the brief I was defending. The lesson for
this team, and I would put it in the converged spec: **when two documents
disagree, the answer is an owner ruling, never a majority vote among
documents.** I came one step short of that and recommended the wrong winner.

**What the corrected table changes in the rest of this proposal:**

- **§1 and §2:** the visit-count differentiator is gone. Optimal is four full
  visits; the incumbent advertises four. I have rewritten defensible claim 3 to
  rest on *what a visit is* rather than *how many*. Anyone who drafted copy
  against six-and-nine must redraft.
- **§2, claim 5 and §3, device 5:** a calculator that computes in the open is a
  *stronger* transparency signal than a flat price, because it shows the
  arithmetic rather than asserting fairness. But it must never gate the number
  behind an email field, and its two sliders must have plain-language labels a
  bereaved person can parse on a phone.
- **§6e:** resolved. The light/heavy split is rejected outright, which means the
  prior art's "every visit is a full visit" is now the ruling and there is no
  vocabulary conflict left. See §6e.
- **§6k:** the arithmetic changes to 160,000 ֏ ÷ 4 = **40,000 ֏ a visit**.
- **Pricing IA:** every proposal now needs room for **five** products, not four,
  with a calculator. A four-card row does not fit the business. This is a
  layout consequence and the UX and system slots need to hear it before they
  converge.

**What is still open, and someone must rule before the tariffs page is drawn:**

1. **Do the four fixed products carry any size-based surcharge, or is the
   calculator exclusively how Special is priced?** `CLAUDE.md` said "flat single
   price for every client, no plot-size surcharge"; `FINAL-CONTENT` §4.3 carried
   per-m² and per-monument surcharges. The 26.08 ruling reinstates a calculator
   but I have not been told which of those two models it computes. The copy
   changes completely depending on the answer — "flat, whatever the size of the
   plot" is either our strongest pricing sentence or a false claim.
2. **Is the repeat-Express rule alive at any price?** FINDINGS #3 records it
   cancelled on 25.08; the corrected table does not mention it. Silence is not
   a ruling.
3. **Where does the 95,000 ֏ figure from `FINAL-CONTENT` decision #8 stand?**
   It was tied to the old numbers. If it survives, it is subject to the ban on
   discount framing; if it does not, its strings must be deleted rather than
   left in the string file for a copywriter to find.

### c. The hero's central asset does not exist, and the brief does not know it

The brief requires GPS/verified-reporting proof on the first screen. The
photographs are placeholders; the professional shoot is planned "during the
September pilot" (`CLAUDE.md` TODO 1). The design of the most important screen
on the site is therefore being specified around an asset that will not exist
until after this work is delivered.

Everyone will paper over this with stock photography. **I want stock
photography banned outright, as a hard rule, and I want it in the converged
spec.** A stock cemetery photograph is by definition a picture of a stranger's
grave, used to sell a service to a bereaved family. It is a moral liability and
it will be recognised as one. The current build already demonstrates where this
road ends: FINDINGS #30 found a stock photograph of a nurse in scrubs with a
stethoscope on a memorial-plot care site.

**My proposal:** design the hero around **the report's structure, not its
photographs.** The metadata strip — date, cemetery, plot, crew, `GPS confirmed`
chip, "8 photographs · 2 videos" — is real, renderable from data today,
truthful, more specific to us than any image, and it degrades honestly: the
photographic frames are a replaceable layer that goes from neutral placeholder
to real September imagery without a redesign. It is also, incidentally, the
more differentiated choice. Every competitor in the category leads with a photo
of a clean headstone. None of them leads with the evidence record.

**Every proposal should be tested for this: what does it look like with no
photographs?** If it collapses, it cannot ship in September.

### d. "One script per locale" collided with the brief's own locked table — now resolved

**Original finding:** the brief ruled that the English site carries no Armenian
script anywhere, while the same document mandated prices in `֏` — U+058F, which
sits in the Armenian Unicode block — and named the products in Armenian.

**Resolved by ruling 2:** no Armenian *words* in the English and Russian
locales; `֏` appears in all three because it is a currency sign, not copy. That
is the right cut and it is cleanly implementable.

Two things still follow from it that the design slots must action:

- **`֏` needs its own element and its own font stack**, because nobody has
  verified that Ghea Mariam or Montserrat Arm carries U+058F. If the glyph is
  missing, the failure must be one character wide, not a broken price. FINDINGS
  #21 shows this already happening on the live build, where the dram sign falls
  back to a system face and renders visibly smaller and lighter than the digits
  beside it. **That is a rendering bug in the most important string on the
  site** and it is nobody's slot unless the system lead claims it.
- **The English and Russian product names must now be written**, because
  "Optimal" and "Օպտիմալ խնամք" can no longer share a page. `FINAL-CONTENT`
  §4.2 flags four of the five Armenian forms as `[OPEN]` and unconfirmed
  against a superseded price list. The Armenian names need owner or localiser
  confirmation and the EN/RU names need inventing. Nobody owns this task.

### e. "Light visit" and "heavy visit" were in the brief's table and on the stop-list — now resolved

**Original finding:** the locked table described products as "2 heavy + 4 light"
while `FINAL-CONTENT` §3.2 banned `light visit` and `heavy visit` from
client-facing copy and ruled that every visit is a full visit. Both could not
hold, and "light visit" was bad copy besides — a buyer paying six figures reads
"light" as "the one where they do less".

**Resolved: the light/heavy split is rejected outright.** The prior art's rule
now stands unopposed and becomes a positioning asset rather than a copy
constraint — see §2, defensible claim 3, where "every visit is a full visit"
is now doing the work the visit count used to do.

One consequence to carry into the spec: **Inspection is not a visit type, it is
a survey**, and with the grade vocabulary gone there is a risk it gets absorbed
into the same word as everything else. Its card must say plainly that **no
cleaning is done on that visit** and that what arrives is a documented
condition report and a **priced quote** for recommended work. That quote is the
strongest thing about the product and the brief buries it in a sub-clause.

### f. The brief has no conversion model, so nothing in it prioritises the form

The brief specifies sections, colours, contrast, typography and a long list of
prohibitions. It never states what the page must *produce*: **a consultation
request, from a stranger, on a phone, in roughly ninety seconds.** Because that
is unstated, four specialists will optimise for how the page looks while
scrolling, and the single highest-value surface on the site — a two-field
form — will get the least design attention. It always does.

What the converged spec must say:

- **Two fields. Name, and phone-or-WhatsApp.** Nothing else. No account, no
  payment, no plot address, no dropdown of cemeteries. Every additional field
  is a percentage.
- **WhatsApp as a first-class channel, not a footnote.** The diaspora buyer at
  one in the morning in Glendale will not fill in a form; they will message.
  WhatsApp Business has been live since mid-August. A tapped WhatsApp link is a
  legitimate conversion and should be given equal visual weight to the form on
  mobile.
- **The callback promise visible in the form**, verbatim and identical
  everywhere it appears.
- **A consent checkbox as a separate control**, with a link to the privacy
  policy — required by the bank, required by the developer spec with date/IP
  capture, and currently absent entirely (FINDINGS #9).
- **UTM capture into HubSpot** (portal 149057498, `PROJECT-MEMORY-FULL.md` §8)
  is an acceptance condition for the platform and has design consequences: the
  form must survive a query string and the success state must not lose it.
- **The form may never sit inside a dark band** (see g).
- Success state in place, on the page, with what happens next and a name to
  call. Not a redirect to a thank-you page nobody reads.

### g. The dark-band rule and the error colour create a trap the brief states but does not resolve

The brief says the consultation form may never sit inside a dark band, because
the error colour `#8C3A2E` measures 2.12 on Dark Olive and is invisible. It
also asks for a closing CTA — which on a page like this is the single most
natural place for the one full-bleed dark band, and which contains the form.
Somebody is going to design exactly that and it will fail review at the end.

**Resolve it now, one line in the spec:** the dark band ends above the form.
The closing section may be dark; the form sits on Ivory, inside it or below it,
on a light ground. Or pick a light-on-dark error treatment and document it. I
prefer the first — a light form panel inside a dark closing band is also the
better design, because it makes the form the brightest object on the page at
the moment of decision.

### h. Motion is requested as an aesthetic, with no purpose attached

"Modern, beautiful, professional, motion included" is a mood, not a brief. On a
site about graves, motion that does not demonstrate something reads as
decoration on a grave — and the audience is 35–60, on mobile data, abroad.

**The only motion that earns its place here is motion that demonstrates the
product:** the report assembling from its components; the before→after
comparison under the reader's own control inside the report artifact; the GPS
point resolving; the family circle populating with accounts; **the calculator
recomputing in front of the reader**, which under the corrected pricing is now
the single best motion opportunity on the site, because the motion *is* the
transparency argument.

Predicted failure, and it is nearly certain: scroll-jacked parallax, an
animated generative hero (**the current build already loads
`vanta.clouds.min.js` from an unpinned CDN**, FINDINGS #32), and count-up
number animations — for numbers that do not exist, on a site whose defining
sin was inventing them. Kill all three in advance, in writing, and require
`prefers-reduced-motion` parity in every proposal rather than as a note.

### i. The before/after ambiguity is inherited, not resolved

The brief forbids the before/after slider because the after-image must never be
the opening image. But the report protocol is *built* on four before and four
after from identical points, and the world's proven content format for this
category is before/after. A specialist could reasonably read the brief as
banning the thing that is our core evidence.

**Ruling:** before/after is the *report's internal grammar* — it belongs inside
the report artifact and inside video content, where both frames are visible
simultaneously and labelled with dates. It is never the marketing hero, never a
draggable slider, and no caption may imply neglect by the family rather than
the passage of time and weather. The distinction is between *documentation* and
*a reveal*. We do documentation.

### j. Nobody has said what the site claims about a portal that does not exist yet

The site will describe a client portal and Family Circle in the present tense.
The platform is being built by an outside contractor with target readiness
~20.09, and the September pilot runs without online payment, on bank transfer
against a short contract. If the site launches promising a portal that is not
live, the first promise a paying customer meets is broken — on a site whose
entire proposition is that our promises are checkable.

**Ruling needed:** what is true on launch day. My proposal — the report is
described as *delivered to you and forwardable by a plain link*, which is true
whether the portal is live or the report arrives as a link by email; the portal
and Family Circle are described in the present tense only once they exist, and
until then Family Circle is described as *what a subscription includes*, which
it does, without asserting a screen a visitor could ask to see today.

### k. "Premium" is asserted everywhere and priced nowhere

160,000 ֏ (roughly $410, and any such figure must be marked approximate) sits at
the top of the American annual range and is a large multiple of the local
four-visit norm. A local buyer can arrange something with a caretaker for cash.
Nothing in the brief's section list is responsible for justifying the gap, so no
specialist will own it.

**The justification must be visible, and it must be arithmetic, not adjectives:**
160,000 ֏ across four visits is **40,000 ֏ a visit**, and every visit is a full
visit of the whole plot and every monument, with a documented report after each
one. That is a defensible number stated plainly, and it converts the
"expensive" objection into a comparison the reader runs themselves. It is not a
discount and must not be dressed as one — the prior art's ban on
`save` / `only` / `just` / struck-through prices stands. **Give it a slot in the
pricing section.**

**The corrected pricing makes this section more load-bearing than it was.**
40,000 ֏ a visit is a bigger per-visit number than 30,000, against an incumbent
offering the same visit count. The per-visit arithmetic is now an argument we
have to *win* rather than one we can simply state, and it is won on the
definition of a visit and on the evidence that follows it — not on the number.
If the copy lead cannot make that case in three sentences, the pricing section
is where this site loses.

### l. Smaller, but they will each cost a review cycle

- The brief's Inspection composition is "photo/video of condition + priced list
  of recommended work". That priced list is **a quote**. Say so. See §6e.
- Tokens must not encode hex values in their names (`--sky-blue`, not
  `--a4d6e8`), so the contested Sky blue is genuinely a one-line change.
- The slogan is not a heading and must never be used as an `h1` (see §5.3).
- Nothing in the brief bans exclamation marks or emoji. The prior art bans
  both, in every language and every state. Carry that forward — it will
  otherwise reappear in the Armenian copy, which is where it always does.
- Four of the five Armenian product names are still `[OPEN]` in the prior art
  and were attached to a superseded price list. They need confirmation, not
  inheritance (see §6d).

### What I expect each of the other four to get wrong

- **Visual identity:** will use Olive as a text or button colour because it is

  forget-me-not as a decorative motif scattered across the page rather than a
  mark. Will produce a warm, soft, photographic hero that is indistinguishable
  from a hospice, a spa, or a Scandinavian skincare brand.
- **Layout / UX:** will draw four equal pricing cards in a row — which is now

- **Motion:** parallax, scroll-jacking, count-up numbers, an animated hero

- **Copy:** will drift into consolation and rhetorical questions; will write

## 7. Tone of voice, concretely

### Five sentences we would write

1. **"The crew recorded the GPS point at your plot on 14 September. The report
   has eight photographs, two short videos and the crew's note."**
   Every element is checkable by the reader against a document they hold. This
   is the register: fact before feeling, and the photographs carry the feeling
   so the words do not have to.

2. **"160,000 ֏ AMD a year — four visits, 40,000 ֏ a visit, and every visit is
   the whole plot and every monument."**
   Defends a premium price with arithmetic instead of adjectives, and answers
   the local buyer's real objection without ever acknowledging it as an
   objection. Note what it does *not* claim: not "flat whatever the size of the
   plot", because the surcharge question in §6b is still open, and I will not
   write a sentence the calculator might contradict.

3. **"We started in 2026. We have no reviews to show you and we will not borrow
   anyone else's."**
   The admission is the asset. It is also structurally uncopyable — an
   incumbent cannot say it — and it inoculates the reader against the absence
   of social proof before they notice it themselves.

4. **"Care is rarely one person's decision, and it should not be one person's
   inbox."**
   Sells Family Circle by describing a family the reader recognises. Names no
   audience, no country and no distance, and works identically for the son in
   Glendale and the daughter in Nor Nork.

5. **"If we damage the monument, we repair it at our cost."**
   A commitment with money attached to it. For a company with no history, a
   liability we accept in writing is the only trust that can actually be
   purchased — and it is short enough that nobody can accuse it of hedging.

### Five sentences we would never write

1. **"When was the last time you visited?"**
   Makes the reader say their own absence out loud. It wounds the diaspora
   buyer and insults the local one, and the research pass shows this audience
   arrives already defended against guilt — dozens of forum threads where the
   community's whole function is to absolve. Guilt does not convert here; it
   closes the tab.

2. **"The only service in Armenia with GPS-verified visit reports."**
   Falsifiable in one search by exactly the person we most need to convince.
   See §1 and §2.

3. **"Peace of mind, delivered."**
   A feeling we are not entitled to promise, in a register we have banned, with
   nothing in it a reader can check. It would fit any insurance company in the
   world, which is the test it fails.

4. **"Trusted by hundreds of Armenian families across the world."**
   Zero paying customers. It is also, near-verbatim, what the current build
   ships alongside three fabricated testimonials illustrated with photographs
   of real public figures — the sentence that is the reason this rebrand exists.

5. **"She would have wanted it this way."**
   Puts words in a dead woman's mouth to close a sale. If there is a line in
   this category, it is this one.

## 8. Ranking criteria for the design lead

Ordered. Apply in sequence; a proposal that fails an earlier test does not get
credit for a later one.

1. **Truth gate — binary, disqualifying.** Does any element assert something
   untrue as of today: a count, a testimonial, a review, a year, a claim of
   being first or only, a portal screen that does not exist, a guarantee not
   yet legally bound, **a price from the superseded table**? If yes, the
   proposal is out regardless of how good it looks. This is the whole reason we
   are rebuilding.

2. **Checkability of the first screen.** On a 360px phone, within one screen,
   does a stranger encounter at least one concrete verifiable thing — a date, a
   GPS confirmation, a price, a named promise, a real phone number? A mood is
   not a pass. Rank proposals by how much checkable substance survives the fold.

3. **Does the report carry the most visual weight on the page?** Measure it:
   area, vertical position, contrast against its neighbours. The report is the
   product. If pricing, the hero image, or a values section outweighs it, the
   proposal has the business backwards.

4. **The swap test — specificity.** Replace every word with lorem and show it
   to someone. Could it be a Berlin dental practice, a Copenhagen skincare
   brand, or a Series-A SaaS? If yes, it has no identity. Require each proposal
   to name **three elements that could only belong to a company that
   photographs graves in Yerevan for families who are not there.** If the
   author cannot name three, there are none.

5. **Distance to the form.** Count taps from first paint to a submitted
   consultation request on a phone, and count how many screens have a
   reachable CTA. The winner should be two taps or fewer from any point in the
   page, with WhatsApp equally reachable.

6. **Measured compliance, not asserted.** Contrast ratios computed, not
   claimed; 16px minimum body on mobile; 44×44 hit areas; visible focus; every
   hover affordance duplicated for keyboard and touch; and every component
   shown with **real Armenian and Russian strings at their real lengths**, not
   with English placeholders.

7. **Degradation without photography.** Render each proposal with neutral grey
   frames where photographs go. If it stops working, it cannot ship before the
   September shoot — which is to say, it cannot ship.

8. **Commercial completeness — see §6a.** Does the proposal make room for
   About, a full five-product tariffs route, contacts and a registration number
   in every footer, legal restrictions, AMD prices with an FX note, an English
   privacy policy, a refund policy and service-delivery terms? A rebrand that
   cannot be submitted to Ameriabank has not delivered the thing the business
   needs, and it delays card revenue past October.

9. **Cost of a token change.** Sky blue is contested. Changing it — or the
   dark, or the display face — should be one line in one file. Ask each
   proposal what it costs; a proposal that cannot answer has no system. Apply
   the same test to a price: the 26.08 ruling proves prices move, and a
   proposal where changing 160,000 to something else means re-cutting a graphic
   is a proposal that will be wrong within a month.

10. **Tie-break, and it is a real test:** which of these pages would Davit be
    willing to send, unaccompanied, to a stranger whose mother died in March?
    If two proposals are otherwise equal, that question separates them and it
    separates them correctly.
