## Patterns (3–5)

1. **Find a Grave / BillionGraves coverage of Yerevan is near-zero** (~400
   memorials across all Yerevan cemeteries vs. hundreds of thousands of
   graves; Tokhmakh 9% photographed, no GPS). Diaspora genealogists using
   the world-standard tools hit a wall exactly at Armenia — and the
   photo-volunteer system fails without local volunteers. MemoryCare's
   Զննում (20,000 ֏ inspection with photo/video + GPS lock-in) is almost a
   paid, guaranteed version of an unfulfillable Find a Grave photo request.
2. **The pain is documented first-person**: the 2019 "Lost and Found in
   Armenia" account (huge unmapped cemeteries, no staff, no directory,
   relief at receiving GPS coordinates "within hours") is exactly the
   emotional arc of MemoryCare's diaspora persona — usable as copy research,
   not as testimonial.
3. **HUSH.AM is the real incumbent on the genealogy funnel**: a 1M-grave
   search database (since ~2015, press-covered, app on Google Play, RU/EN/HY)
   that upsells flowers + a 4-visit/year photo-report care package.
   MemoryCare wins on service depth (6–9 visits, heavy cleaning, video+GPS,
   client portal, family circle) but loses on the "first I must FIND the
   grave" entry point — the Զննում tariff should be positioned to capture
   exactly that entry intent.
4. **Search intent in Russian ("как найти могилу родственника в Ереване")
   currently resolves to hush.am's FAQ** — an SEO/content opening for a
   MemoryCare RU-language "how to find and care for a relative's grave in
   Yerevan" page.
5. **Organized diaspora tombstone-photo projects exist but are tiny**
   (Armenian Genealogy Forum's Cemetery/Tombstone Projects: 4 topics, 21
   posts) — better treated as goodwill/partnership channels than as demand
   or competition.
